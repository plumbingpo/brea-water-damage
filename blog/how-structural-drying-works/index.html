How Structural Drying Works in Brea, CA

Home / Blog / How Structural Drying Works in Brea, CA

Ask people around Brea, CA what restoration involves and drying the structure rarely comes up, although it is the core of the work. Once the visible water is gone, a lot is still held in the building itself, in framing lumber, drywall, insulation, subflooring, and concrete. Mold, lingering smells, and deteriorating materials are the result when the trapped water is not taken out correctly. Here we cover how professional drying works and the reasons it outperforms a do-it-yourself effort by a wide margin.

Psychrometry: The Science Behind Drying

Psychrometry, a science concerned with gas-vapor mixtures and their thermodynamic and physical properties, is what structural drying is built on. Put simply, it is about the way air movement, relative humidity, and temperature work together to pull water from wet materials. IICRC-certified technicians use psychrometric calculations to determine the equipment setup each drying situation needs, including the type and number of dehumidifiers, the positioning of air movers, and the target temperature and humidity levels.

The Equipment Professionals Use

Professional drying gear is in a different class from anything sold for home use. A residential dehumidifier pulls 2 to 3 pints of water from the air each hour, while an LGR (Low-Grain Refrigerant) unit can pull 15 to 30 or more. High-velocity air movers create precisely directed airflow at 2,500 CFM or more to speed evaporation from wet surfaces. Desiccant dehumidifiers are used for specialized situations, including cold-weather drying and extreme moisture loads. The dollars invested in a full set run to tens of thousands, and it only performs properly in trained hands.

Each Stage of Professional Drying

Professional structural drying follows a systematic process. It begins with thermal imaging and moisture meters, which are used to map every area the water reached. This map guides equipment placement and establishes baseline moisture readings. Next, psychrometric calculations decide the position of each air mover and dehumidifier so that water evaporates and is removed efficiently. Adding heat is an option when a quicker evaporation rate is needed. A technician returns daily to take moisture readings at all monitoring points, adjust equipment as needed, and document progress. The job is not finished until every wet material tests at an acceptable level against a dry reference standard.

What to Expect for Drying Time

Expect 3 to 5 days for the majority of residential jobs when proper commercial gear is used. The timeline depends on several factors: the volume of moisture present, the types of materials affected (concrete dries much slower than drywall), ambient conditions, building construction, and the number and type of equipment deployed. As a rule, standard drywall needs 2 to 3 days, wood framing 3 to 4 days, and concrete 5 to 7 days or more. You will get a projected finish date from the initial readings, and it is revised as the structure dries.

Why Daily Readings Matter

Without a daily check, drying is only a guess. Nobody can say that materials are drying, keeping to the expected rate, or safely dry unless a calibrated instrument has measured them. A wall or floor may seem dry under your hand and still be dangerously wet deeper inside. Verifying it takes a moisture meter that has been calibrated. Professional drying documentation also gives your insurance company proof that proper industry protocols were followed, supporting your claim and protecting your property value.
